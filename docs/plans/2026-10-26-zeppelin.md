# Zeppelin plan, 26–27 October 2026

The next blueprint: a rigid airship the size of the airliner at its default scale, lifted by Create Aeronautics' hot
air for real, steered and driven by physics, flown from a control car under its bow by its own autopilot, sharing the
airliner's transponders, maps and voice, and packed with things to find. A panel of five agents explored it first:
physics (Aeronautics and Sable decompiled), code reuse, real airships, discovery for young players, and flight
gameplay. Their full reports are in the session's scratchpad (`panel/physics.md`, `history.md`, `discovery.md`,
`flight.md`). The physics this plan rests on is summed up in the appendix, so the plan stands without them.

## Ground rules for the night

- **Branch:** everything lands on `main`, never pushed. One atomic commit per step, Conventional Commits subjects,
  each reviewed with the `review-changes` skill first. A `chore(release): bump version to 0.1.0-alpha.N` follows every
  build that gets play-tested (from alpha.102).
- **The airliner must not change.** Every refactor in milestone 1 is behaviour-free and checked in game before the
  next step: build, take off, land on a runway and at a pin.
- **Children:** every cue also gets a picture, a colour or a sound, never text alone. Nothing a child can do strands
  them: the zeppelin has no fall inside it that hits a floor, and no fall out of it without a canopy (decision 9).
- **Config:** the zeppelin and what its blueprint lays are not experimental. Experimental behaviour goes behind a
  switch (wind drift, the ship's cat), off by default unless Jan says otherwise (decision 10).
- **Other mods:** Aeronautics is named only by registry id (`aeronautics:adjustable_burner`, the Hot Air Burner, and
  `aeronautics:<colour>_envelope`), in `AerolargeConfig` and resolved in `AirframePalette`. Sable stays inside
  `SableFlight` and `SableClient`.
- **Testing:** in the dev client with Blockpilot (see Testing), headless where that is enough. Whatever could not be
  verified in game is said plainly in the outcome.
- **Questions** that come up in the night are decided by the panel (physics, engineering, design for young players),
  majority vote, logged below.

## The zeppelin as a player meets it

**Size and look.** 145 blocks long and 30 across at its widest, a third of the way back: a blunt nose and a long
taper to a pointed tail, about 72 m by 15 m, the size of the airships that fly sightseeing trips today. It is always
built at 2 blocks a metre, whatever the airliner's `scale` says. Outside it wears a silver fabric cover cut to its
curve like the airliner's skin, blue girder rings showing at the nose cap, four fins in a cross at the tail with
rudders and elevators that visibly swing, four streamlined engine cars on struts with propellers that spin with their
thrust, and a glazed control car under the bow. Navigation lights, a tail light and red beacons are the airliner's.

**Two blueprints** in the creative tab, under the airliner's: the **Zeppelin Blueprint** (the airship with its decks
empty, to build in) and the **Furnished Zeppelin Blueprint** (fitted out, with everything to discover). Both use the
same preview miniature and refusal rules as the airliner's, and `/aerolarge build <pos> <dir> zeppelin [furnished]`.

**Layout,** nose (x = 0) to tail (x = 144), in blocks at scale 2:

- **Gas cells (x 10–122):** eight cells, one per bay between the girder rings, each a balloon of Aeronautics
  envelope blocks in its own rainbow colour just inside the silver cover. Their burners stand on the cell's floor
  behind iron grilles, glowing up into them. The colours are the zeppelin's map: "meet at the green balloon".
- **Keel corridor (x 6–130):** a matted catwalk along the bottom under the cells, its floor striped in the colour of
  the cell overhead, with round portholes up into every cell, lit orange when its burners roar.
- **Ring shafts** at three rings (between double bulkheads, so they belong to no cell): stair cores from the keel to
  the engine cars' crawl-ways, and amidships the **Sky Shaft** up to the **Sky Deck** on the crown.
- **Control car (x 18–38):** under the bow on a short neck from the keel: the control room (rudder wheel facing
  forward, elevator wheel facing port, burner lever, two engine telegraphs, the autopilot's control head, the
  transponder, a flight display, a map and a camera display), then the navigation room, then an observation lounge with
  a glass floor and a wide rear window. Its wheel is the main landing wheel.
- **Passenger decks (x 40–78), in the belly:** the hull's curve gives the promenade windows their slant. A deck:
  promenades, a dining room, a lounge with the piano, cabins. B deck: galley, washrooms, crew mess, and the boarding
  stairs that fold down out of the belly.
- **Drop room (x 80–96):** a railed slot in the floor looking straight down, with the parachute rack, the jump light,
  the ballast taps and, later, the cloud car and the trapeze.
- **Aft (x 96–144):** the mail and freight room, crew bunks, the tail cone with the Owl's Nest (see Discoveries), the
  lower fin's bumper wheel.

## How it flies

Verified in decompiled code by the physics panellist unless marked; the numbers and classes are in the appendix.

- **Lift:** hot air. Each cell's burners fill it with gas, each m³ lifting 1.5 kpg (Sable's mass unit; a block weighs
  1, our `super_light` blocks 0.25) times the air pressure at the gas. Pressure thins with height, so the zeppelin
  settles where lift meets weight, at about y = 63 + 250·ln(1.5·gas/mass). The burners' level is the height, with no
  scripted lift anywhere. A furnished zeppelin should weigh 5,000–9,000 kpg (to measure). Four burners in each of eight
  cells give 16,000 m³ at most, half as much again as the heaviest estimate needs at y 200.
- **Driving the burners:** the airliner's thrust relays, unchanged (`Relays.drive`). A burner's output is its redstone
  signal over 15, and the relay powers every side. Every burner stays at signal 1 or more (a pilot flame), so its
  balloon is never dropped and rebuilt.
- **Pitch trim:** each cell is its own balloon with its own centre, so tilting the burner levels toward the bow or the
  stern trims the nose. One block of centre-of-mass error tilts it about 5°, so trim is always on; two signal steps
  take that block out.
- **Height hold:** a feed-forward of the gas that floats its mass at the height asked (mass from Sable), plus a slow
  correction on the height error and climb. Hot air answers in 1.5 to 9 seconds, so the zeppelin feels heavy and
  deliberate, and climbs early before hills.
- **Ballast:** water tanks whose state sets their Sable mass. Dropping one is an instant, physical climb, and the
  autopilot's last resort.
- **Thrust:** Create Propulsion creative thrusters in the engine cars, two ahead and one astern in each, on the
  throttle's relay channels and one astern channel per engine, with the propeller drawn spinning by Aerolarge. The
  eight ahead at full drive it at about 28–38 blocks a second, so Full (25) has room to spare. Aeronautics' propeller
  bearings are physical too, but need 64 to 100 sail blocks each to match, so the spike decides whether a real
  propeller of sensible size earns a place (decision 2).
- **Steering:** differential thrust; ahead on one side and astern on the other turns it on the spot. The rudders are
  the airliner's drawn control surfaces, real lifting blocks but weak at airship speeds.
- **On the ground:** it rests light on its wheels and bumpers, the burners holding nine tenths of its weight, so the
  buoyancy keeps it upright. Sable has no wind, so a parked or hovering zeppelin stays where it is. Mooring to a mast
  (next night) is a Sable constraint from the nose to the mast.
- **Rules for the gas cells** (from how Aeronautics fills a balloon):
  - Nothing that is not airtight in a cell's walls or ceiling. A window or door there lets the gas out down to it.
  - Ways into a cell come from below, through an open mouth; the gas stays above a hole in its floor.
  - Each burner has a clear line straight up to its cell's ceiling, and a cell's burners share one level and one flat
    strip of ceiling.
  - Nothing that opens (doors, hatches, gear) in a cell's walls, and no airtight block that ever changes near one.
    Relays and lamps changing state inside a cell are harmless: only airtightness counts.
  - Bulkheads are whole, or neighbouring cells join into one and the trim is lost.

## Architecture

From the reuse panellist's map of the code. The rule: one build pipeline and one autopilot block entity, each told
which craft it serves by a saved `Kind`.

| System | Verdict | The change |
|---|---|---|
| `AirframeBuild`, `Placement`, `Fitting`, gluing, `PhysicsAssembler` | Reuse | `Placement.of` takes a `Layout` interface instead of `AirframeLayout`; the build's messages per craft. |
| Shell pass (`SnappedShell`, `shapeAt`, `inward`) | Seam | Takes a `Hull` surface (`AirframeShape.HULL`, `ZeppelinShape.HULL`); `NOSE_LENGTH`, `windscreenAt` and `assemblerSpot` move behind it. Proven unchanged for the airliner by hashing its cell map at scales 1–4 before and after (a scratch check, not committed). |
| Fit-out classes (`CabinFitOut`, `UpperDeck`, `ScreenArc`, ...) | Airliner-only | The zeppelin's fit-out is its own; it reuses their blocks, not their code. |
| `AutopilotBlockEntity` | Seam | Saved `Kind` (missing loads as `AIRLINER`), a package-private `Pilot` interface with `AirlinerPilot` as a thin adapter over today's methods (no code moves) and a new `ZeppelinPilot`. `Kind.limits()` carries the speed range and steps (read by `set`, `AutopilotSettingsScreen` and `AutopilotDeskArt`); `Kind.scale()` is the config's for the airliner and 2 for the zeppelin. `Phase` names are reused (take-off is weigh-off and up ship, land is coming straight down). |
| Control head, its screen, `SetAutopilot`, `OpenAutopilotScreen`, `UnstickAirliner`, destinations, pins | Reuse | `OpenAutopilotScreen` carries the kind (protocol bump). |
| `Relays` channels | Seam | Append `LIFT` and `ASTERN_1` to `ASTERN_4` (saved by name, so older saves load). The pilot sets each `LIFT` relay by its place along the hull, which is how the trim tilts. |
| Transponders, radar, tower, flight strips | Seam | Append `MarkerIcon.AIRSHIP` (protocol bump; check it fits the transponder screen's grid), call sign ZEP, strips that know a hovering craft. |
| `DestinationMap`, `MovingMap`, `RouteMap`, `PilotHud`, passenger screens | Reuse | They draw the craft from the autopilot's data. |
| `OutsideCamera`, `CameraMount`, `PassengerCameras` | Seam | Camera places per kind, the kind carried in `SableClient.Frame`; enum names kept (they are saved). |
| `Callouts`, `CabinAnnouncements` | Reuse | New flight-deck lines with Kokoro (works on macOS). |
| `AtcRadio` | Seam | `Kind.talksToTower()`, false for the zeppelin tonight: new tower lines need Windows' speech synthesis. The tower still shows it on radar. |
| `Turbulence`, `AirPockets`, `Decompression`, `FlightStrip` thresholds | Reuse | They act above 20 blocks a second, so a zeppelin is spared, which is right. `FlightStrip` reads anything under 15 as taxiing, so it learns the kind to show hovering. |
| `PinLanding`, `RunwaySearch`, `RunwayCheck`, `AirlinerShape`, `Hop`, gear | Airliner-only | `GroundSurvey` is reused to check the ground under a zeppelin coming down at a pin. |
| `FrameProtection`, `AirlinerSpawnGuard` | Seam | Envelopes and burners are protected only inside a zeppelin's frame (`#aerolarge:unbreakable_in_frame`, read by the autopilot's framing), never every player's balloon; envelopes join `#aerolarge:airframe` so nothing spawns in the dark cells. |
| `SableFlight` | Grows | `mass` and `centreOfMass` (`getMassTracker()`); later `moor`/`unmoor` (`addConstraint` to the world). |
| `BlueprintPreview` | Seam | A `Blueprint` interface (miniature state, middle height) in place of `instanceof AirlinerBlueprintItem`. |

**New classes:** `airframe/` `Layout`, `Hull`, `ZeppelinShape` (metres, nose north: profile, rings, cells, keel,
control car, engine cars, fins, wheels), `ZeppelinLayout` (blocks), `ZeppelinFitOut` (the furnished decks and the
discoveries); `autopilot/` `Kind`, `Pilot`, `AirlinerPilot`, `ZeppelinPilot`, `Buoyancy` (the gas law: the
feed-forward and the trim split); `block/` the cover panels (the airliner's skin pieces in silver fabric, their own
blocks), `HelmBlock` (rudder and elevator wheels), `TelegraphBlock`, the burner lever (modelled on the thrust
lever), `BallastTankBlock`, the cell porthole (glass Aerolarge tags `#aeronautics:airtight`, spike test 3);
`item/` `Blueprint`, `ZeppelinBlueprintItem`; data: the ballast's mass by state (`physics_block_properties`,
generated), the tags above.

**Controls are fly-by-wire:** the wheels, the burner lever and the telegraphs set what `ZeppelinPilot` asks of the
burners and engines while the autopilot is off; engaged, it moves them itself where everyone can see.

**Network and saves:** protocol `"20"` → `"21"` (the kind in `OpenAutopilotScreen`) → `"22"` (`MarkerIcon.AIRSHIP`).
NBT: `Kind` and the zeppelin pilot's own compound default when missing; relay channels are only appended; no existing
key changes.

## The night, in order

Each step is a commit (or a few) that leaves the build green and the airliner flying as before. Milestones 0–4 are the
night's core; milestone 5 is as far as time allows, in its order.

### 0. Physics spike (nothing committed)

In the dev world, with `/fill` and `/sable assemble`, a test balloon of white envelopes about 40×20×20 with four
burners on thrust relays (powered by hand):

1. Balloon forms on the assembled sub-level; goggles on a burner read the volume (screenshot).
2. Height law: `sable info` for the mass, set the signal, `sample sable:info <sub> brief every 20t x120`, compare the
   settling height with the formula.
3. A glass porthole in a wall leaks; a block of ours in `#aeronautics:airtight` does not (tag added in a scratch
   datapack); an open mouth in the floor keeps the gas.
4. Two cells behind a bulkhead stay two balloons, and a fore/aft split tilts the body as predicted.
5. A creative thruster pushes it, and an Aeronautics propeller bearing on a creative motor started after assembly
   either thrusts or does not (decision 2).
6. The flood fill's cost when burners first light (server tick, and client frame time with the heat glow).
7. Save and reload in the air: the gas comes back, no drop.

**Gate:** if balloons do not form or hold on a sub-level, switch to the fallback in the appendix (Aerolarge's own
data-only lifting gas) and log it; the layout and pilot change only in what they place and drive.

**Test:** build it, walk it end to end with screenshots (floating or misplaced blocks, the cells' colours, the

### 3. Flying it

1. `feat(block)`: the burner lever, the two engine telegraphs (Stop, Slow, Half, Full and Astern as a hand, a snail,
   feet, a rabbit and a backward arrow, each with its bell), the rudder wheel with its compass and the elevator wheel
   with its bubble level, the fins' panels swinging with them. Fly-by-wire through `ZeppelinPilot`'s manual mode.
2. `feat(autopilot)`: `ZeppelinPilot`:
   - **Weigh-off:** burners up until it rests light, a level balance picture and a chime.
   - **Up ship:** straight up at 1–2 blocks a second with the bell and a "Up ship!" callout; engines only 15 blocks
     clear of the ground.
   - **Cruise:** height above the ground (40 by default, never under 20) or above sea level, capped short of the
     ceiling it works out; speed on the telegraph steps (-5 to 25 blocks a second); heading by differential thrust.
   - **Hover at the pin**, nose toward it, creeping in, then holding still.
   - **Circle** a landmark slowly.
   - **Come down at the pin:** survey the ground (`GroundSurvey`), sink at a block a second, rest light on the wheels.
   - Unattended for a minute: hover, then come down where it is and rest.
   - The autopilot screen's limits by kind (protocol 21).
3. `feat(sounds)`: the burners' roar at the cells, the telegraph bells, the up-ship bell, the callouts (Kokoro).

**Test:** a whole trip with Blockpilot sampling `sable:info` a second at a time: weigh-off, up ship, cruise to a
destination on the map, hover over a pin, circle, come down at a pin. The settled height against the formula, the
trim within a degree or two, no oscillating height.

### 4. Its place in the airliner's world

3. `docs(readme)`: a Zeppelin section, as a player meets it.

**Test:** the zeppelin and an airliner on the tower's radar and each other's maps at once; the cameras.

## Outcome

What a player meets now (alpha.104):

- **Building it:** a Zeppelin Blueprint and a Furnished Zeppelin Blueprint in the creative tab, with a see-through
  Model Zeppelin showing where and which way it will build; `/aerolarge build … zeppelin [furnished]` for operators.
- **The airship:** 490 blocks of silver cover over seventeen blue rings, sixteen rainbow gas cells of real Create
  Aeronautics hot air on 113 burners, four engine cars with spinning propellers, the fins, the control car under the bow,
  the passenger decks with their promenades, the gangway, the boarding stairs.
- **Flying it with the autopilot:** take-off straight up, climbing, cruising, turning on the spot, flying to a pin and
  landing on it, landing where it is, resting on its wheels with a bell, all held level by burning the cells fore and
  aft differently; its ceiling worked out from its mass. The settings screen's dial runs 0–25 blocks a second.
- **Flying it by hand:** the rudder wheel, elevator wheel, two engine telegraphs and burner lever in the control car,
  which also show what the autopilot does.
- **Things to discover:** the Sky Shaft up a rope to a railed Sky Deck on the crown, a rope into a balloon's hot air to
  a gallery among the burners, the glass-floored lounge in the control car, and on the furnished one cabins with bunks,
  a galley with a coffee machine, a dining room, a lounge with a bar and bean bags, and armchairs along the promenades.
- **Its place in the world:** an airship marker on every map and the tower's radar; the gas cells and burners can't be
  broken where the blueprint laid them; it stays loaded once assembled; a safety canopy opens over anyone falling

## Appendix: physics notes

From Create Aeronautics 1.3.2, Simulated 1.3.2 and Sable 2.0.6, decompiled with Vineflower. Sable's mass unit is the
kpg; gravity is 11 blocks/s².

- **Balloon volume** (`BalloonBuilder.upwardsBiasedFloodFill`, `completeBalloon`): from under the first airtight
  block straight above a burner (`HotAirBurnerBlockEntity.doRaycast`, up to 80 blocks), filling sideways (4 ways) and
  up, never down; lower layers join only if sealed. A non-airtight block in the ceiling kills the cell; one inside the
  gas takes 1 m³ off. `#aeronautics:airtight` is the envelopes plus wool, and tags add up.
- **Gas** (`ServerBalloon`): output = the burner's scroll (5–500 m³, default the maximum) × signal/15; the gas moves
  toward it with a 9 s time constant, about 1.5 s for small changes. Lift = 1.5 kpg/m³ × gas × air pressure at the
  gas centre, applied at the centroid of the whole capacity, with a restoring pressure-gradient term.
- **Air pressure:** exp(−0.004·(y − 63)), smoothed to 0 between 280 and 320: 1.0 at 63, 0.71 at 150, 0.58 at 200,
  0.47 at 250. Equilibrium y ≈ 63 + 250·ln(1.5·G/m); +10 % gas ≈ +24 blocks.
- **Rebuilds** (20–50 ms on the server and again on every client, by an offline port of the fill): a burner lighting
  with no balloon, an airtight block placed or removed within the balloon's bounds + 24, the highest burner changing
  layer, assembly. Steady state costs O(1) a tick. Gas is saved (`BalloonLevelSavedData`).
- **Mass:** the sum of `sable:mass` over colliding blocks (non-colliding blocks weigh 0); `super_light` is 0.25 at
  priority 1001; mass updates cheaply as blocks change. Estimated 5,000–9,000 kpg furnished. Burners for neutral
  buoyancy at 9,000: 12 at y 63, 21 at y 200.
- **Levitite:** −9 kpg net per block, does not thin with height, never climbs by itself, strong friction below
  3 blocks/s. Not used; never with full weight-cancelling on top of hot air.
- **Thrust:** a creative thruster gives 10,000 at setting 99 and signal 15, linear in signal. A propeller bearing
  gives 0.2·S^1.5·RPM × pressure (S sails): 26,000 at 64 sails and 256 RPM. A small propeller gives 256. Envelope
  drag is linear, about 2,100–2,850 per block/s for the whole ship (estimate).
- **Stability (estimates):** pitch and roll are stable while the gas centre is above the centre of mass (about
  6–8 blocks here); yaw is neutral; a 1-block centre-of-mass offset tilts it about 5°. Turning at 1°/s needs about
  2,400 of thrust difference. It stops in about 10 s with the engines off.
- **Sable hooks:** `ServerSubLevel.getMassTracker()`, `RigidBodyHandle`, `getOrCreateQueuedForceGroup(ForceGroups…)`,
  `SubLevelPhysicsSystem.get(level).getPipeline().addConstraint(subLevel, null, …)` for mooring, and data-only
  `floating_materials` with `physics_block_properties` (per-blockstate mass overrides for the ballast).
- **No wind** anywhere in Sable or Aeronautics; Aeronautics' only gust is the jet from a punctured balloon.
- **Fallback lift:** `data/aerolarge/floating_materials/lifting_gas.json` (lift 1.5, scaled with pressure) on a
  non-colliding, massless gas block filling each cell, with trim blocks whose state scales their lift. The same
  equilibrium, no flood fill, no burners.
