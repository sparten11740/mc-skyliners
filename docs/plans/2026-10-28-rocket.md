# Rocket plan, 28–29 October 2026

The third blueprint: a rocket as tall as the airliner is long, with stories one above another, an elevator stopping at
each, a glass cockpit in its nose, driven by Create Propulsion's thrusters. It is flown by setting a pin on the
autopilot's map: it climbs almost straight up, leans a little toward the pin, falls back over it and catches itself on
its thrusters, landing on its fins. Three explorations of the code came first (how a craft plugs in, thrust and
physics, the systems to reuse); what they found is summed up here, so the plan stands without them.

## Ground rules for the night

- **Branch:** everything lands on `main`, never pushed. One atomic commit per step, Conventional Commits subjects,
  each reviewed with the `review-changes` skill first. A `chore(release): bump version to 0.1.0-alpha.N` follows every
  build that gets play-tested (from alpha.160).
- **The airliner and the zeppelin must not change.** Every generalisation is behaviour-free for them and checked in
  game before the next step.
- **Reuse before writing anew:** the rocket takes the autopilot, its screen and map, the build pipeline, the thrusters
  and relays, the glass cockpit, the cameras and displays, the livery selector, the airstairs, seats and rooms from the
  airliner and the zeppelin, generalised where they assume a horizontal craft, never copied.
- **Children:** every cue also gets a picture, a colour or a sound, never text alone. Nothing a child can do strands
  them, and nothing a ride does hurts them: the rocket never falls freely with them inside (see Physics).
- **Config:** the rocket and what its blueprint lays are not experimental, so on by default; each piece has a switch.
- **Testing:** in the dev client with Blockpilot. Whatever could not be verified in game is said plainly.
- **Measure, never claim:** every number in this plan marked *to measure* is measured before it is relied on.

## The rocket as a player meets it

- **Size:** as tall as the airliner is long, 72.7 m at the configured scale (145 blocks at the default 2), about 20
  blocks across, a nose cone on top, four fins at the base that are also its landing legs.
- **Getting aboard:** the airliner's airstairs from a door in the entry story, a few stories up.
- **Inside:** a lift up a central shaft with a stop and a lift panel on every story: the entry story, a cargo bay, the
  galley and mess, the bunk room, a greenhouse, a swimming pool, a techno club, a lab, the observation deck; seats on
  every story for the climb and the landing, under seatbelt signs.
- **The cargo bay** holds a rover, lowered to the ground down a ramp out of her side and driven by remote control.
- **The lab** has a cage with a tardigrade in it the size of an elephant.
- **The cockpit,** in the glass nose: pilot seats, the autopilot's control head, the curved row of screens (flight
  display, map, systems, camera), the large camera display and, beside it, a livery selector.
- **Liveries,** six, picked on the selector: the comic-book moon rocket's red and white checks, white and black with
  roll stripes, silver with red fins and nose, and the airliner's colours, polar red and midnight blue with gold stars
  fitted to her. Painted like the zeppelin, by tint, no block changing.
- **Flying to a pin:** pin dropped on the map, a ten-second countdown spoken, shown on the screens in big numbers and
  by lights; lift-off in a roar of exhaust; she climbs nearly straight up, leaning at most about 10° toward the pin,
  coasts over it, falls back and catches herself with a landing burn, settling on her fins near it. Without a pin she
  hops straight up to the set height and comes back down where she was.

## Physics, as far as it is known

- **Weight:** Sable weighs a body as the sum of its blocks' mass (`#sable:super_light` 0.25 kpg each); gravity is 11
  blocks/s². Shell, floors and fittings come to about 12,000 blocks: 4,000–5,000 kpg dressed (*to measure*).
- **Thrust:** a creative thruster gives 10,000 at full, so about six hover her; twelve to sixteen in clustered bells
  give a thrust-to-weight of about 2.5 (*the hover signal to measure*).
- **Upright:** a body pushed from below does not stay upright by itself. Side jets near the top and the bottom fired
  together move her sideways, fired against each other tilt her; the pilot differences her tilt each tick (Sable gives
  no angular rate) as the zeppelin's does her pitch.
- **Riders:** she never falls freely: on the way down she accelerates at most about 0.6 g, so whoever stands on a
  floor stays on it, and the landing burn presses them down rather than meeting them as they float.
- **Height:** Sable removes a body only above y = 100,000; above the build height there is only sky. Clients track a
  body within 320 blocks.
- **Staying loaded:** a force-load ticket like the zeppelin's; land ahead works for her horizontal travel only.

## Milestones

Each ends in a commit, a build and a play-test, and is said done only when seen working.

0. **Spikes.** A short test rocket: the hover signal, lift-off, holding upright on side jets, a 50-block hop and a
   landing. Create's elevator inside an assembled body, parked and hovering. A player standing aboard through the
   climb, the descent and the landing burn: drift, falling through floors, damage. If the elevator fails, the
   quick-lift (decision 4).
1. **The airframe:** `RocketShape`, `RocketLayout` with pieces, the blueprint, `Craft.ROCKET`, assembly, `/aerolarge
   build rocket`, Blockpilot's `aerolarge:build rocket` and an `aerolarge:rocket` query for sampling.
2. **A hop without a pin:** countdown, lift-off, climb, hold, descent, landing burn, settling on the fins.
3. **To a pin:** the lean, the coast, falling back over the pin, the landing burn there; staying loaded; switched off
   in the air she holds her height and then lands under herself.
4. **The cockpit:** the glass nose, the screens, the camera display and the livery selector, her cameras, her pictures
   on the panels and the HUD.
5. **The stories:** floors and windows, the lift with a stop per story, the airstairs, seats and seatbelt signs, then
   the rooms in the order of decision 5.
6. **Sound and voice, README, version, desktop.**
